WHY DISTRIBUTE IT?

From a tabletop process to a routed control loop

The original MiniWater testbed made tanks, tubing, pumps, and dye visible in one place. The SPHERE adaptation keeps its raw-water → treatment → delivery sequence while separating control stages across facilities. That lets a demonstration expose network boundaries, data age, signal ownership, and the difference between a controller’s reading and modeled process state. The historical images below show the original setup; the replay on this page is a software preview.

MEET THE SYSTEM

Control stages across two facilities

The installed Rockwell ControlLogix runs the raw-water stage at one facility. At another, two arm64 Raspberry Pis running OpenPLC handle treatment and delivery. In tank 2, PLC2 fills, assesses reported color, doses blue or yellow, settles, and declares treatment complete. A software process model currently supplies sensor readings and consumes pump, valve, and doser commands.

4
RED TEAM / BLUE TEAM

Choose what you would check

The 2024 classroom CTF paired malicious code or opportunistic commands with defender code vetting and PLC-value monitoring. This poster version uses two bounded scenarios: a false PLC1 level and a manipulated PLC2 dosing decision. The public page presents their trace only.

ATTACK A

False raw-water level

PLC1 receives a level below modeled truth. Its reported range stays above the 10 cm handoff threshold, holding off transfer as tank 1 fills.

Defender cue

Compare reported and modeled level and range, plus the transfer command, over time.

ATTACK B

False color, changed treatment

PLC2 receives a yellow color reading higher than modeled truth. It can skip needed yellow dosing and raise treatment complete while the model remains off target; PLC3 then sees the handoff.

Defender cue

Compare the reported and modeled yellow values, the doser command, PLC2 complete signal, and delivery handoff.

Which observation gives the defender independent evidence?
